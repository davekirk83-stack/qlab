# Standing rule — come at problems to solve them, not to kill them (DK, 6 Nov 2026 17:52)

DK: "I want you to finally take note about your approach to killing off ideas, and to come at problems with an intention to solve them, please."
Earlier the same day: "you're doing the 'rapid test kill off' thing I've warned you about. You're not trying to solve it or apply new thinking or techniques to behaviours which we know exist." / "We know that markets spike and drop erratically. This is predictable."
(Also stated 19 Oct: do not write off ideas with potential when hitting barriers; ask "if I was doing this, how would I make it work?" — not death by testing.)

For every research task in this repo:
1. Start from the behaviour. If it plainly exists (spikes, snap-backs, trend days, sweeps), the question is HOW to capture it, not WHETHER it is real.
2. Study it before writing a rule: when it happens, its shape, what separates the cases that work from the ones that don't.
3. Build it the way practitioners trade it (scale-in, staged exits, regime gating, exits first), on enough data.
4. A failed first version is a design problem to fix, not a verdict. Iterate on the development half; judge once on the untouched holdout.
5. A result goes on the settled list (doc 182) only after a genuine attempt to make it work has failed — and the entry says what was tried.
6. Report what looks exploitable first, and what would make the weak parts work.
